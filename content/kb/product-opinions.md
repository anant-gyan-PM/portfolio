## Anant's Favorite Product — PhonePe
Anant's favorite product is PhonePe, the Indian digital payments app. He admires how it's made digital payments effortless for millions of people in India — splitting bills, paying utility bills, buying insurance, or investing in gold, all in a few taps — while remaining approachable even for people who aren't very tech-savvy. He values it for being fast, reliable, and genuinely built for everyday users, and prefers it over competitors like Paytm or Google Pay because of its consistency: he's rarely experienced a failed payment, and the interface stays clean and focused rather than cluttered with banners and cross-sold services. If he could improve it, he'd add simple personal-finance insights (spending patterns, small savings or investment suggestions based on habits), tools for small merchants to analyze transaction patterns and repeat customers, and cross-border or travel-friendly UPI payment options as digital payments expand globally.

## How Anant Would Design a Global Crypto Payroll Product
Anant would design a global crypto payroll product around three layers: (1) a stablecoin-based settlement layer (e.g. USDC or PYUSD) to eliminate volatility and enable instant cross-border transfers, (2) a compliance layer integrating KYC/AML APIs and local tax-reporting features so the product stays compliant in every country, and (3) an experience layer — an intuitive employer dashboard for paying global teams with real-time conversion tracking. He'd start with a single high-friction payment corridor (e.g. US to Latin America or US to India) to validate adoption before scaling regionally, eventually adding auto-conversion to local fiat, tax withholding, and payroll-system APIs. The goal: make paying in crypto feel as easy and compliant as paying in dollars.

## Anant's View on the Biggest Challenge in Crypto Payroll
Anant believes the biggest challenge in crypto payroll isn't the technology — it's regulation and user trust. Every country treats crypto differently (currency, asset, or taxable event per transaction), which makes confident, at-scale rollout hard. On the user side, volatility concerns and uncertainty about safely cashing out remain real barriers. His approach: design compliance-first so the product automatically adapts to local rules, and give employees the option of choosing how much of their salary to receive in crypto versus fiat, building comfort and adoption gradually rather than forcing full conversion.

## Crypto Use Cases That Excite Anant Beyond Trading
Beyond trading, Anant is most excited by cross-border payroll and microfinance — using stablecoins to let freelancers or workers in emerging markets get paid instantly, a real quality-of-life change. He's also interested in tokenized rewards and on-chain reputation systems, which could make gig-economy work fairer and more transparent. In his view, trading drove the first wave of crypto adoption, but real-world payments infrastructure is what will drive mainstream utility — crypto as infrastructure for global financial inclusion, not just speculation.

## How Anant Prioritizes a Product Roadmap
Anant starts roadmap decisions from the customer and the problem, not a feature wishlist. He looks at funnel data, usage patterns, support tickets, and direct customer conversations to identify problem themes, then sizes each by potential impact: does it unblock a large share of users, meaningfully improve activation/retention/trust, or reduce operational risk? He weighs that against engineering effort and risk to find high-impact, deliverable work, and deliberately protects space for less glamorous but essential work — reliability, compliance, and tech debt — because that work protects customer trust. When something doesn't clearly tie to a top customer problem or strategic goal, he's comfortable cutting it or turning it into a lightweight experiment instead. At CryptoSmartlife, this approach led him to push leadership to reprioritize a broken onboarding flow ahead of new asset listings, based on funnel data showing it was the real barrier to verified-user growth.
